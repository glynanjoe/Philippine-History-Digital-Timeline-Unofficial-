JULY 7, 1892 · SPANISH COLONIAL PERIOD

The Founding of the Katipunan

The Katipunan was a secret revolutionary organization founded to unite Filipinos in their struggle for independence from Spanish colonial rule.

Historical Background

On July 7, 1892, Filipino patriots established the Kataastaasan, Kagalanggalangang Katipunan ng mga Anak ng Bayan, commonly known as the Katipunan. The founding took place in Manila following the arrest and exile of José Rizal, which discouraged hopes for peaceful reform.

Among its founding members were Andrés Bonifacio, Deodato Arellano, Ladislao Diwa, Teodoro Plata, and Valentín Díaz. The organization operated secretly because Spanish authorities opposed movements seeking independence.

Its goal was to unite Filipinos and achieve freedom from Spain. The Katipunan recruited members and spread its ideals of patriotism and national independence.

Historical Significance

The Katipunan became a major force in the Philippine Revolution, which began in 1896. Its members helped transform the desire for reform and freedom into a revolutionary movement against Spanish colonial rule.

The organization played an important role in the development of Filipino nationalism and the continuing struggle to establish an independent nation.

Connection to the Present

The story of the Katipunan teaches the value of unity, courage, responsibility, and love of country. It also reminds us that people can work together to seek meaningful change and protect their rights.
