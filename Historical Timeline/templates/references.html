References and Historical Sources

This page lists sources used to research the 14 events in the Philippine historical timeline. Primary sources provide original documents or eyewitness accounts, while secondary sources help explain and interpret historical events.

1. Early Philippine Societies and Barangays

Period: Before 1521

  1. Juan de Plasencia. (1589). Customs of the Tagalogs. The Philippine Islands, 1493–1898, through Project Gutenberg .

    An early Spanish-era account of Tagalog customs and social organization. It must be read critically because it reflects the author's colonial perspective.

  2. University of the Philippines Diliman Main Library. The Philippine Islands, 1493–1898. Collection information and access options .

    Describes a major collection of translated Spanish-era documents useful for researching early Philippine history.

2. Arrival of the Magellan Expedition

Date: 1521

  1. Antonio Pigafetta. Account of the First Voyage Around the World. Read a translated edition through Project Gutenberg .

    Pigafetta participated in the expedition and recorded observations about its voyage and encounters. His account is an important eyewitness source, though it reflects the expedition's perspective.

  2. Emma Helen Blair and James Alexander Robertson, editors. The Philippine Islands, 1493–1898. University of the Philippines Diliman Library .

3. Blood Compact

Date: 1565

  1. Emma Helen Blair and James Alexander Robertson, editors. The Philippine Islands, 1493–1898. University of the Philippines Diliman Library .

    This collection provides historical documents from the Spanish colonial period that can be consulted for accounts of early Spanish-Filipino encounters.

  2. National Commission for Culture and the Arts. Philippine History Resources. NCCA website .

    Use relevant historical articles to compare accounts of the blood compact and its colonial context.

4. Establishment of Spanish Rule in Cebu

Date: 1565

  1. Blair and Robertson, editors. The Philippine Islands, 1493–1898. University of the Philippines Diliman Library .

    A collection of Spanish-era accounts relevant to the Legazpi expedition and the establishment of colonial settlements.

  2. U.S. Department of State. Self Study Guide: The Philippines. Read the historical overview (PDF) .

5. Manila–Acapulco Galleon Trade

Period: 1565–1815

  1. Blair and Robertson, editors. The Philippine Islands, 1493–1898. University of the Philippines Diliman Library .

    Contains colonial-era records useful for investigating trade, colonial administration, and connections between the Philippines and the Spanish Empire.

  2. U.S. Department of State. Self Study Guide: The Philippines. Read the historical overview (PDF) .

6. Founding of Manila

Date: 1571

  1. Blair and Robertson, editors. The Philippine Islands, 1493–1898. University of the Philippines Diliman Library .

    Provides access information for primary historical documents about Spanish colonial expansion and Manila.

  2. U.S. Department of State. Self Study Guide: The Philippines. Read the historical overview (PDF) .

7. Cavite Mutiny

Date: January 20, 1872

  1. John N. Schumacher, S.J., and Nicholas P. Cushner, S.J. (1969). Documents Relating to Father José Burgos and the Cavite Mutiny of 1872. Read through Ateneo de Manila University .

    Examines documents related to the mutiny and helps compare historical accounts of its causes and aftermath.

  2. Emma Helen Blair and James Alexander Robertson, editors. The Philippine Islands, 1493–1898. University of the Philippines Diliman Library .

8. Execution of GOMBURZA

Date: February 17, 1872

  1. John N. Schumacher, S.J., and Nicholas P. Cushner, S.J. (1969). Documents Relating to Father José Burgos and the Cavite Mutiny of 1872. Read the scholarly article .

    Provides documentary context for the events connected with Father José Burgos and the 1872 executions.

  2. National Commission for Culture and the Arts. Philippine History Resources. NCCA website .

9. Founding of the Katipunan

Date: July 7, 1892

  1. The Light of Liberty: Documents and Studies on the Katipunan, 1892–1897. Katipunan documents and studies. Katipunan Documents and Studies .

    Provides access to historical Katipunan documents and research, including materials from the revolutionary movement.

  2. National Commission for Culture and the Arts. In Focus: Andres Bonifacio and the 1896 Revolution. Read the historical overview .

10. Philippine Declaration of Independence

Date: June 12, 1898

  1. Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista. Act of the Declaration of Philippine Independence. June 12, 1898. Read the declaration .

    The original declaration is primary evidence of the revolutionary government's claim to independence from Spain.

  2. National Commission for Culture and the Arts. The Philippine-American War (1899–1902). Read the historical overview .

11. Inauguration of the First Philippine Republic

Date: January 23, 1899

  1. Government of the Philippines. Proclamation No. 533, s. 2013. January 9, 2013. Read the proclamation .

    Officially recognizes January 23 as Araw ng Republikang Filipino and describes the inauguration of the First Philippine Republic.

  2. Philippine Centennial Documents. Historical documents from 1898 to 1912. Browse the document collection .

    Includes materials related to the Malolos Congress and the early Philippine Republic.

12. Japanese Occupation of the Philippines

Period: 1942–1945

  1. United States Holocaust Memorial Museum. Holocaust Encyclopedia and World War II historical resources. Browse the encyclopedia .

    Offers broader World War II context. Use Philippine-specific records to verify local events, dates, and experiences.

  2. Ateneo de Manila University. American Historical Collection. Explore the collection .

    Describes an archival collection containing historical documents and photographs related to the Philippines and its international relationships.

13. Philippine Independence from the United States

Date: July 4, 1946

  1. Governments of the Philippines and the United States. Treaty of General Relations Between the Republic of the Philippines and the United States of America. July 4, 1946. Read the treaty through the Supreme Court E-Library .

    The treaty is primary evidence of the formal transfer of sovereignty and the establishment of relations between the two countries.

  2. U.S. House of Representatives, History, Art & Archives. The Philippines, 1898–1946. Read the historical overview .

14. EDSA People Power Revolution

Dates: February 22–25, 1986

  1. President Corazon C. Aquino. Proclamation No. 3, s. 1986. March 25, 1986. Read Proclamation No. 3 .

    This primary source records the new government's stated commitment to a transitional government and constitutional reform following the revolution.

  2. Encyclopaedia Britannica. People Power Revolution. Encyclopaedia Britannica .

    Use the relevant article to check the chronology and historical interpretation of the revolution.

Research Verification

Historical dates and claims should be compared across primary documents and credible secondary sources. Primary sources record what a person or institution said or did; they do not automatically establish that every claim in them is accurate. Interpretations should therefore consider the author's purpose, perspective, and historical context.
