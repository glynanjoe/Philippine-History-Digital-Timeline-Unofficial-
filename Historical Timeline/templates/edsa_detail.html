FEBRUARY 22–25, 1986 · CONTEMPORARY PHILIPPINES

EDSA People Power Revolution

The EDSA People Power Revolution was a major event in Philippine history in which millions of Filipinos peacefully demonstrated for political change and helped restore democratic government after years of authoritarian rule.

Historical Background

During the administration of Ferdinand Marcos Sr., the Philippines experienced martial law, restrictions on civil liberties, and human rights abuses. Political opposition grew, especially after the assassination of opposition leader Benigno “Ninoy” Aquino Jr. in 1983.

In February 1986, a snap presidential election was held between Marcos and Corazon “Cory” Aquino. The election was disputed, with allegations of fraud and irregularities. These events increased public calls for political change.

On February 22, 1986, key military officials, including Juan Ponce Enrile and Fidel V. Ramos, announced that they were withdrawing their support from Marcos. Cardinal Jaime Sin encouraged people to support those who had broken with the government. Large crowds gathered along Epifanio de los Santos Avenue (EDSA).

For several days, civilians, religious groups, and other citizens gathered in peaceful demonstrations. Many offered food, prayers, and support to the military defectors. On February 25, 1986, Corazon Aquino was sworn in as president, while Marcos also held an inauguration. Marcos and his family subsequently left the Philippines.

Historical Significance

The revolution brought an end to Marcos Sr.'s rule and opened the way for the restoration of democratic institutions. Corazon Aquino became president, and the government began rebuilding democratic processes, including constitutional reform.

The event became internationally known as an example of mass civic action. It demonstrated the influence of ordinary citizens, religious communities, and groups within the military in shaping the country's political future.

Connection to the Present

The EDSA People Power Revolution continues to remind Filipinos that citizenship involves more than voting. People can help shape the nation by staying informed, participating peacefully in public life, respecting human rights, and holding leaders accountable.

Remembering this event also encourages people to examine historical evidence carefully and recognize the importance of protecting democratic freedoms.
