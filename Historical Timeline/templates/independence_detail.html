JUNE 12, 1898 · PHILIPPINE REVOLUTION

The Philippine Declaration of Independence

On June 12, 1898, Filipino revolutionaries proclaimed the Philippines' independence from Spanish colonial rule, marking an important moment in the nation's history.

Historical Background

After years of Spanish colonial rule and growing calls for reform and freedom, the Philippine Revolution began in 1896. Filipino revolutionaries fought against Spanish authorities to end colonial control.

On June 12, 1898, Emilio Aguinaldo proclaimed Philippine independence in Kawit, Cavite. The ceremony included the raising of the Philippine flag and the performance of the national anthem.

The declaration expressed the revolutionary government's claim that the Philippines was a free and independent nation. However, Spain did not recognize this declaration. Later in 1898, Spain ceded the Philippines to the United States under the Treaty of Paris, creating another struggle for Philippine independence.

Historical Significance

The declaration symbolized the Filipino people's determination to govern themselves. It became a major milestone in the development of Philippine nationalism and the establishment of a Filipino-led government.

Although the declaration did not immediately secure internationally recognized independence, it remains an important symbol of the country's struggle for freedom.

Connection to the Present

The Philippines celebrates Independence Day every June 12. The occasion honors those who fought for freedom and encourages Filipinos to value their national identity, rights, and responsibilities as citizens.
